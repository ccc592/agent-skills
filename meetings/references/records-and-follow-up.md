# Records and follow-up

## Build notes from evidence

Use supplied notes/transcripts and authorized linked artifacts. Capture date,
purpose, actual attendees when known, source coverage, reviewed document/version,
and draft status. An invite list does not prove attendance. Mark missing audio,
speaker ambiguity and uncertain wording; do not fill gaps with plausible dialogue.

Summarize by topic, preserving relevant rationale, alternatives, dependencies,
dissent and unresolved risks. Avoid a verbatim dump. Use source timestamps or
paragraph references for consequential claims when available. Attribute views
only as needed and supported; do not include unnecessary personal details.
Do not turn jokes, tentative remarks or a manager's suggestion into a commitment.

| Record type | Admission rule |
|---|---|
| Decision | Explicit outcome and authorized decision process/owner supported by source; otherwise tentative/unconfirmed |
| Agreed action | Work the meeting actually agreed to; scope and accepted owner evidenced separately |
| Timing | Agreed date or window only; preserve relative date ambiguity/time zone if unresolved |
| Proposal | Suggested work or outcome lacking agreement; no implied assignment |
| Open question | Unresolved issue with its consequence and known dependencies |

An agreed action can have an unconfirmed owner or date: show those fields as
unconfirmed rather than inventing values or silently deleting the action. “I can
look” is an offer, “we should” a proposal, and “maybe Friday” tentative timing.
“Alex, do this” is a requested assignment unless acceptance or the applicable
assignment process is established. Evidence of agreement can come from explicit
read-back or the organization's documented process; do not assume every team
uses consensus. Never erase dissent because a decision owner made a valid call.

## Review and distribute

Produce the shared record and a separate private organizer draft. Suggest missing
clarifications, proposed conversations, and follow-up options in the private
artifact; label every suggestion and unsupplied owner/date as proposed. Avoid
speculative judgments about motives, diagnoses or employee performance. A private
label alone does not restrict storage permissions. Keep confidential matters out
of broadly visible files; verify intended location and audience.

Use the team's correction window if one exists, or propose one without claiming
it was agreed. Silence during review does not establish consent or consensus.
A later clarification should be marked as such and linked to the original
record; a new decision is not a retroactive rewrite of the meeting.

Before authorized external writes, confirm recipients, destination permissions,
exact version, and scope. For tasks, preview only agreed work and accepted
assignments; a request to clean up notes is not permission to create tickets.
Inspect existing items to avoid duplication. Report actual sends/writes with
links or identifiers and verify results; do not claim success from a draft or
an uncertain API response. Calendar cancellations and edits need authorization too.

Track agreed actions using existing tools where requested. Report status from
current evidence, not optimistic inference. Proposed reminders, escalation or
future meetings require the normal authorization for their actual execution.
